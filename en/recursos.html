IDSA

What are the “5 IDSA core assets”?

The International Data Spaces Association (IDSA) sums up its work in five core assets. Its members apply them in more than 200 data spaces, such as EONA-X or Catena-X. Real-world experience flows back to IDSA as use cases and improvements, and its experts take it to ISO/IEC and CEN/CENELEC, where it becomes a standard.

1
Core asset 1 of 5

IDS Reference Architecture Model (IDS-RAM)

It defines the roles, the components (connector, catalogue, identity provider, vocabularies) and the layers of a data space, with three cross-cutting perspectives: security, certification and governance. Version 4 is stable; version 5 is in draft.

2
Core asset 2 of 5

Dataspace Protocol (DSP)

It specifies how connectors communicate: catalogue publication (DCAT), contract negotiation with ODRL policies and the transfer process. Since 2024 it has been governed by the Eclipse Dataspace Working Group; version 2025-1 is stable, has a compatibility test kit and is progressing at ISO as ISO/IEC 26450.

Core asset 3 of 5

IDSA Rulebook

It brings together the functional, technical, operational and legal requirements for creating and running a data space. It separates what is mandatory from what is optional and introduces the data space governance authority. The 2026-1 edition is the current reference.

Core asset 4 of 5

Data Space Body of Knowledge (DSBoK)

It is the shared knowledge underpinning IDSA's professional qualification programme, from the Fundamentals level to the business and technical consultant profiles. It is taught by authorised training providers; in Spain, by SQS, with exams managed by BAIDATA.

5
Core asset 5 of 5

IDS Certification

It certifies connectors and operational environments for functionality, interoperability and security, with reference to IEC 62443. It has assurance levels, starting with a checklist-based self-assessment, and with the Dataspace Protocol 2025-1 it adds automated interoperability testing.

Standardisation

From IDSA assets to standards

The reference model, the Rulebook and the Dataspace Protocol feed into ISO/IEC 20151, 26450 and 26451 and into the work of CEN-CENELEC JTC 25, which has already published EN 18235-1:2026 on trusted data transactions.
